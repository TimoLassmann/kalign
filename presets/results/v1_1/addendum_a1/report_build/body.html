REPORT · authoritative for the v1.1 re-fit and the 3.6.1 comparison · checked 2026-10-07

The numbers come from the run's tables (presets/results/v1_1/report/tables/) and the Addendum A1 rows on the workstation. The new presets are not yet compiled into Kalign; the command-line program still ships the 3.7.0 presets. Where this page and the tables disagree, the tables are right.

Across 6,141 reference alignments, Kalign 3.7.0 needed {SPEEDUP_RANGE} less single-thread CPU than Kalign 3.6.1 in every mode. Its protein default and accurate presets were also more accurate (SP +0.044 and +0.040), its protein recall preset was 0.005 lower in SP than the separate 3.6.1 recall ensemble at half the CPU, and nucleotide SP stayed within 0.004 of 3.6.1. A pre-registered re-fit by grouped five-fold nested cross-validation then replaced three of seven presets (protein default, protein accurate, nucleotide recall): each raises out-of-fold TC by 0.011 to 0.015 within 1.2 times the 3.7.0 CPU, and protein precision by 0.04 to 0.06. The protein presets lose SP on alignments below 20% identity (−0.025) and on PREFAB (−0.013). Final-fit scores exceed the out-of-fold estimates by at most 0.006 in SP and TC for protein, so the fitting procedure shows no meaningful overfitting. MUSCLE 5 and MAFFT L-INS-i remain more accurate on protein (SP 0.796 and 0.784) at about 18 and 2.4 times the CPU of Kalign accurate.

1Reference data and leakage control

The re-fit used two pools of reference alignments, matching Kalign's two sequence types. Every file is downloaded from its original source and checked against a recorded SHA-256, 28 exact duplicates were removed, and no simulated alignments were used.

Related cases share a fold. Protein cases are linked when a reference sequence of one is identical to, or contained in, a reference sequence of the other; nucleotide cases are linked by length-dependent sequence identity and by Rfam clan. Whole groups were assigned to five folds, stratified by benchmark and by the identity bin of the reference (protein 543 groups, largest 15.0% of cases; nucleotide 1,976 groups, largest 17.1%).

CAVEAT

Grouping protein cases at 50% identity put 51% of them into one group through shared PDB chains, so the stricter rule was used. As a consequence, 921 of 2,916 protein test cases (31.6%) have a homolog of at least 50% identity in another fold. The report splits every protein result by this flag; the re-fitted presets gained no more on those cases than on the others.

2Nested cross-validation and preset search

{SCHEMATIC}

The procedure, from the frozen plan to the decision. Boxes in slate are repeated once per fold; ochre boxes are the two hash-recorded freezes and the frozen decision rule. Every rule, seed and search setting was fixed before the first fit, and the out-of-fold rows were hashed before the final fit started.

For each fold, presets were fitted on the other four folds only and then aligned on every case of the held-out fold. The scores pooled over the five held-out folds estimate the performance of the procedure and are the numbers this report gives. The presets that would ship come from running the identical procedure once on the whole pool; parameters were never averaged across folds.

Each fit drew a training core from its training folds by fixed hash order: at most 5 cases per group for protein and 25 for nucleotide, 840 protein cases including a guard set of 40, and all capped nucleotide cases (1,931 to 2,488). NSGA-III (pymoo 0.6.1.6, population 56, 56 Das-Dennis directions, seed 1234) maximised mean SP, TC and precision and minimised total CPU, with means taken per benchmark and benchmarks weighted equally. Stage 1 searched single-run presets and stage 2 ensembles of three or five runs. Every candidate was screened on 300 cases, and each stage's final front was re-scored on the full core. A stage stopped when its hypervolume improved by less than 0.1% over five generations.

A fixed rule then chose one preset per mode: within 1.2 times the CPU of the 3.7.0 preset of that mode, the non-dominated candidate with the highest mean z-score of SP, TC and precision, where recall must also reach the SP of accurate. A new preset replaces 3.7.0 only if, out of fold, both SP and TC change by at least −0.005, at least one of them by +0.005 or more, and its CPU re-timed at 4 workers stays within 1.2 times 3.7.0.

6Search convergence and Amendment v1.1

In the first run (v1), stage 2 stopped at its cap of 40 generations in all 12 fits while the hypervolume was still rising. A dated amendment, written before any further computation, raised the cap to 140 and continued every fit from its saved state; nothing else changed. In v1.1 every stage-2 search met the stopping rule, after 58 to 119 generations.

10Limitations of the comparison

The bootstrap resamples cases, not groups, so the intervals may be slightly narrow; the decision rule uses point estimates only. Protein leakage control works at the level of identical or contained sequences, and nucleotide leakage control at the level of sequence identity and Rfam clan; relatedness beyond that is not measured. Rfam seed alignments are curated rather than derived from structure. CPU measured under load is 5 to 30% above the unloaded value, which is why the decision used a separate re-timing at 4 workers. The selection rule weights SP, TC and precision equally and can trade SP for precision, which the decision rule then guards out of fold. These properties may limit the generalisability of the comparison to other reference sets.

11Draft text for the paper

Methods

We re-fitted Kalign's presets on two pools of public reference alignments, 2,916 protein cases (BAliBASE 3, PREFAB 4, OXBench, SABRE, BaliFam100) and 3,225 nucleotide cases (Rfam 15.1 seed alignments, BRAliBASE II, MDSA), after removing 28 exact duplicates. Cases sharing a reference sequence (protein) or linked by sequence identity or Rfam clan (nucleotide) were grouped, and whole groups were assigned to five folds stratified by benchmark and reference identity. For each fold, presets were fitted on the other four folds and evaluated on every case of the held-out fold; the pooled held-out scores estimate the performance of the fitting procedure. The shipped presets come from the same procedure applied to the whole pool.

Each fit optimised mean SP, TC and precision and total single-thread CPU with NSGA-III (population 56, seed 1234) in two stages, single-run presets and then ensembles of three or five runs, on a training core of at most five cases per group (840 protein, 1,931 to 2,488 nucleotide cases). Each stage stopped when its hypervolume improved by less than 0.1% over five generations. A fixed rule chose one preset per mode within 1.2 times the CPU of the corresponding 3.7.0 preset, and a new preset replaced 3.7.0 only if its out-of-fold SP and TC each changed by at least −0.005 with one of them improving by at least 0.005. Intervals are 95% BCa bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples). The plan, code, seeds and rules were committed before any fit, the held-out results were hashed before the final fit, and the one later change, a higher generation limit for the ensemble stage, was recorded as a dated amendment before it ran. Kalign 3.6.1, Kalign 3.7.0, MAFFT L-INS-i, MUSCLE 5 and Clustal Omega were run single-threaded on the same cases for comparison.

Results

Kalign 3.7.0 required {SPEEDUP_RANGE} less single-thread CPU than Kalign 3.6.1 across modes and sequence types, 2.5 times in protein accurate, while raising protein SP by 0.040 in accurate and 0.044 in default. The re-fit replaced three of seven presets. Out of fold, the new protein accurate preset reached SP 0.755, TC 0.590 and precision 0.760 against 0.757, 0.579 and 0.717 for the 3.7.0 preset, at 1.16 times its CPU on the training core; the new protein default raised TC from 0.548 to 0.563 and precision from 0.685 to 0.741 at 1.02 times the CPU; and the new nucleotide recall preset raised TC from 0.688 to 0.699. The protein gains were concentrated between 20% and 40% identity and on BAliBASE, OXBench and BaliFam, whereas alignments below 20% identity and PREFAB pairs lost SP (−0.025 and −0.013 for accurate). Final-fit scores exceeded the out-of-fold estimates by at most 0.006 in SP and TC for protein, consistent with little overfitting. MUSCLE 5 (SP 0.796, TC 0.639) and MAFFT L-INS-i (0.784, 0.627) remained more accurate than Kalign accurate on protein at about 18 and 2.4 times its CPU, although Kalign accurate had the highest precision of all programs (0.760).
