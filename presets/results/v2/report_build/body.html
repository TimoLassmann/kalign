REPORT · authoritative for the v2 presets and their comparison · checked 2026-10-07

Tables come from presets/results/v2/ (held-out pooling, Addendum A2 tables, laptop reproduction) and presets/results/v1_1/ (search, Addendum A1). The v2 presets are not yet compiled into Kalign; the command line still ships the 3.7.0 presets. The earlier v1.1 report (presets/results/v1_1/report.html) describes a picking rule that v2 replaced.

A grouped five-fold nested cross-validation over 6,141 public reference alignments produced two presets per sequence type, fast and accurate, picked at the knees of the quality-against-CPU curve by a rule fixed before any held-out number existed. Scored only on cases their search never saw, the protein accurate preset gains 0.028 SP and 0.044 TC over Kalign 3.1, the release of the Kalign 3 paper, and 0.013 SP and 0.023 TC over Kalign 3.7.0; it is level with Clustal Omega on BAliBASE TC, above it on SP, and below MAFFT L-INS-i and MUSCLE 5 at a quarter and a thirtieth of their CPU. The nucleotide accurate preset matches or exceeds every program except MUSCLE 5. Final-fit scores exceed the held-out estimates by at most 0.009, and a fresh laptop build scored with the official bali_score reproduces the BAliBASE numbers to within 0.0003. The speed gain of 3.7.0 is spent on accuracy: at equal accuracy 3.7.0 fast matches the CPU of Kalign 3.1, and the accurate preset costs about 30 times that.

1Changes to Kalign since the Kalign 3 paper

The Kalign 3 paper described release 3.1 (October 2019): SIMD bit-parallel distances (Myers' algorithm with AVX2), guide trees by sequence embedding and bisecting k-means, and one progressive alignment per run. The changes since then, from the project ChangeLog, fall into four groups: ensembles, SIMD acceleration, threading and scheduling, and correctness.

Related cases share a fold. Protein cases are linked when a reference sequence of one is identical to, or contained in, a reference sequence of the other; nucleotide cases are linked by length-dependent sequence identity and by Rfam clan. Whole groups were assigned to five folds, stratified by benchmark and reference identity.

CAVEAT

A 50% identity grouping put 51% of the protein cases into one group through shared PDB chains, so the stricter rule was used; 921 of 2,916 protein test cases (31.6%) therefore have a homolog of at least 50% identity in another fold. In v1.1 the re-fitted presets gained no more on those cases than on the others.

3Nested cross-validation and preset search

{SCHEMATIC}

The procedure. Slate boxes repeat once per fold; ochre boxes are the tagged plan and the hash of the held-out rows (Freeze 2); the green box holds the reported numbers. Search and picking in fold k see only the other four folds, so every case is scored once by presets that never saw it.

For each fold, NSGA-III (pymoo 0.6.1.6, population 56, seed 1234) searched on a training core drawn from the other four folds: at most 5 cases per group for protein (840 cases) and 25 for nucleotide (1,931 to 2,488 cases). It maximised mean SP, TC and precision and minimised total single-thread CPU, first over single-run presets and then over ensembles of three or five runs. Each stage stopped when the hypervolume of its archive improved by less than 0.1% over five generations.

4Picking two modes on the Pareto curve

Amendment v2 picks the presets from each fit's own front with one rule. Quality is q = (SP + TC) / 2 on the fit's training core. A point is accepted when no point costing more gains at least 0.002 in q per doubling of CPU. The accurate preset is the cheapest accepted point; the fast preset is the cheapest accepted point among those using at most one fifth of the CPU of the fastest other aligner on the same cases. Precision is reported but not used, and default and recall become names for accurate.

{SVG:fig_mode_choice_protein}

Protein final fit: the Pareto front (grey points, 415) and its (SP+TC)/2 curve against CPU per case on the 840-case training core, with the two picked presets, Kalign 3.6.1 and 3.7.0, and the other aligners scored on the same cases; the right panel shows precision for the same points. The picks sit where the curve flattens: the accurate preset at 0.48 s per case, the fast preset at 0.044 s, below the fast limit. These are training-core scores and favour the new presets by up to about 0.009.

{SVG:fig_mode_choice_nucleotide}

The same view for the nucleotide final fit (652 points, 2,488-case training core). The curve is nearly flat; an ensemble gains less than 0.002 per CPU doubling, so the rule picks a single run for the accurate preset (0.022 s per case).

The rule's two constants, 0.002 per doubling and one fifth of the fastest other aligner, were set after looking at the final-fit fronts, which are training-core scores; PLAN.md says so. The held-out evaluation applies the rule blind inside each fold, so its numbers include any effect of that choice.

7Speed between releases

Kalign 3.7.0 used {SPEEDUP_RANGE} less single-thread CPU than Kalign 3.6.1 in the same mode, measured on all 6,141 cases with one harness. The exact speed-ups of 3.7.0 leave alignments unchanged; the per-mode ratio also includes preset changes between the releases, which is why protein default gains only 1.1 times (its 3.7.0 preset refines every alignment fully and gained 0.044 SP).

{TABLE:speed}

Against Kalign 3.1 the picture differs: Kalign became slower between 3.1 and 3.6.1 as features were added (single run on BAliBASE: 0.039 s per case for 3.1, 0.064 s for 3.3.5, 0.089 s for 3.6.1 fast), and 3.7.0 returns to the speed of 3.1 (0.034 s) at the same accuracy (SP 0.829 against 0.831). The new presets spend that time on accuracy: the fast preset uses 0.070 s and the accurate preset 1.2 s per BAliBASE case.

Benchmark cases are small, with program start-up a visible share of their CPU. Wall time with several threads and CPU against the number of sequences (hundreds to tens of thousands) are not part of this report; they are the measurements the paper's speed claims need.

8Overfitting estimate

Optimism is the final presets' score on all cases, which include their own training cases, minus the pooled held-out score of the procedure. Values near zero mean the procedure performs as well on unseen cases as on its training data.

{TABLE:optimism}

The largest value is +0.009 SP for protein fast, case-weighted; benchmark-weighted, every value is at most +0.006, consistent with little overfitting.

9Independent reproduction on a laptop

The BAliBASE numbers were reproduced with nothing shared with the workstation pipeline: a fresh build from source on an Apple M3, no cache, the original BAliBASE 3 release from lbgi.fr, and the official bali_score 3.01 with the core blocks of its XML files.

{TABLE:repro}

Mean SP agrees to within 0.0003 for all five presets; the largest single-case differences are BB20002 and BB40002, whose copies differ between the two BAliBASE sources. bali_score reports TC 0.004 lower in every row because of its TC definition. These laptop scores use the final presets, which saw part of BAliBASE in training, so they test reproducibility, not overfitting.

CAVEAT

A native laptop build of Kalign 3.1 without AVX2 scored BAliBASE SP 0.775, far below the 0.831 of the x86 build in section 6; its scalar distance code was a build artefact and that number is not used.

11Limitations of the comparison

The bootstrap resamples cases, not groups, so the intervals may be slightly narrow. Protein leakage control works at the level of identical or contained sequences and nucleotide leakage control at the level of sequence identity and Rfam clan. Rfam seed alignments are curated rather than derived from structure. CPU measured under load is 5 to 30% above the unloaded value, and the Kalign release programs were timed at a lower concurrency than the other arms. The picking rule's two constants were set after inspecting training-core curves. These properties may limit the generalisability of the comparison to other reference sets and larger inputs.

12Draft text for the paper

Methods

We fitted Kalign's presets on 2,916 protein (BAliBASE 3, PREFAB 4, OXBench, SABRE, BaliFam100) and 3,225 nucleotide (Rfam 15.1 seeds, BRAliBASE II, MDSA) reference alignments in a grouped five-fold nested cross-validation. Cases sharing a reference sequence (protein) or linked by sequence identity or Rfam clan (nucleotide) were kept in one fold. Within each fold, NSGA-III optimised mean SP, TC and precision and total single-thread CPU on a training core of the other four folds, first over single-run presets and then over three- and five-run ensembles, until the hypervolume improved by less than 0.1% over five generations. From each fit's Pareto front, two presets were picked by a fixed rule on q = (SP + TC) / 2: accurate is the cheapest point beyond which a doubling of CPU gains less than 0.002 in q, and fast is the corresponding point within one fifth of the CPU of the fastest other aligner. The picks of each fold were aligned on that fold's cases, and the pooled held-out scores, with 95% BCa intervals, are the reported numbers; the shipped presets come from the same procedure on all cases. Plan, code and rules were tagged before the results they govern, and every amendment is dated in the supplement. Kalign 3.1, 3.3.5, 3.6.1 and 3.7.0, Clustal Omega, MAFFT L-INS-i and MUSCLE 5 were run single-threaded on the same cases.

Results

On held-out protein cases the accurate preset reached SP 0.770 and TC 0.602, against 0.742 and 0.558 for Kalign 3.1, 0.757 and 0.579 for Kalign 3.7.0, and 0.761 and 0.593 for Clustal Omega, at 0.95 s CPU per case; MAFFT L-INS-i (0.784, 0.627) and MUSCLE 5 (0.796, 0.639) remained more accurate at 3.7 and 27.6 s. On BAliBASE 3 the largest gains over Kalign 3.1 were in very divergent sequences (RV11, SP +0.061) and internal insertions (RV50, TC +0.142), whereas terminal extensions (RV40) did not improve. On nucleotide cases the accurate preset (SP 0.841, TC 0.701, 0.021 s) exceeded Kalign 3.1 (0.830, 0.680), Clustal Omega and MAFFT L-INS-i, with only MUSCLE 5 higher (0.864, 0.736) at 74 times the CPU. The final presets scored at most 0.009 above the held-out estimates on their own training cases, consistent with little overfitting, and an independent build scored with the official bali_score reproduced the BAliBASE results to within 0.0003 SP. Kalign 3.7.0 produced the alignments of Kalign 3.6.1 {SPEEDUP_RANGE} faster per mode.
